A good name is clear, easy to remember, and easy to spell. It doesn't need to be clever — it needs to work. Test it: say it out loud. Can someone repeat it back and spell it?
Three approaches that work:
Your own name — e.g. "Sarah Cohen Consulting" · Personal, builds trust fast
What you do — e.g. "Clean Space Co." · Descriptive, searchable
The result — e.g. "Bright Books" · Memorable, brand-able
Avoid: hard-to-spell words, initials only, names too generic to stand out

6. How to Get Your First Client — Your Warm 20
Your first client usually comes from a real conversation, not a perfect funnel. Start with the people who already know you, like you, or have seen your work.
Step 1: List 20 people who trust you, respect your work, or could use what you offer.
Step 2: Reach out personally — not a mass message.
Step 3: Share what you do, who you help, and the problem you solve.
Step 4: Ask if they know anyone who could use that support.
Follow up within one week. Most first clients come from the third or fourth touchpoint.

5. Direct Costs — What Each Sale Actually Costs You
Before you can price correctly, you need to know what it costs you every time you make a sale. This is different from your monthly costs — it's the cost per client or per product.
Price − Direct Costs = Gross Profit
What the client pays minus what it costs to deliver = what you actually keep per sale

6. Break-Even & Profit Expectations
Break-even means the business earns enough to cover its costs. Profit means it earns more than it costs to run. Know both numbers before you start.
QuestionFormulaYour Answer
How much do I need to earn per month to cover costs? = Your total monthly costs
How many sales do I need to break even? Monthly costs ÷ Gross profit per sale
When do I expect to break even? Based on your projected sales pace
When do I expect to turn a profit? Revenue consistently exceeds all costs
What profit do I want the business to generate monthly? Revenue − All costs = Profit target

8. Taxes — What to Set Aside
When you are self-employed, taxes are not automatically taken out. You are responsible for setting them aside yourself. Most new founders are caught off guard by this.
Simple starting rule: Set aside 25–30% of every payment you receive.
Put it in a separate savings account before you spend from the business.
Self-employment tax alone is 15.3% (Social Security + Medicare). Income tax adds on top of that.
